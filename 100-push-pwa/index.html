Technique

Your push-up

For this challenge, use the standard push-up. It gives you the best all-round mix of chest, triceps, front shoulders, serratus and full-body bracing without turning the program into a collection of variations.

Rep standard

Every rep should look the same

1 · HandsHands about shoulder-width to slightly wider. Fingers forward or turned out just a little.
2 · BraceSqueeze abs and glutes. Keep one straight line from ears through hips to ankles.
3 · ElbowsLet elbows travel roughly 30–45° away from your torso. Do not flare them straight sideways.
4 · DownLower under control until your chest is close to the floor and your upper arms are at least around parallel.
5 · UpPress the floor away until your elbows reach full extension. Keep your hips and chest moving together.
6 · BreatheInhale on the way down. Exhale as you press up. Keep your neck neutral.
What it trains

A lot more than just chest

CHESTTRICEPSFRONT DELTSSERRATUSABSGLUTESQUADS · BRACING

Your back and biceps are not heavily trained by a push-up. Within the push-up family, this is the broadest default movement for the challenge.

Stop the set when

Your hips sag, your chest stops reaching proper depth, you start craning your neck, or you can no longer lock out cleanly. A clean 8 is more useful than 12 half-reps.

Recalibrate

Max test

Take this test rested, ideally after about 48–72 hours without a hard push-up session. Do as many strict consecutive standard push-ups as you can. Stop when the next clean rep is gone.
